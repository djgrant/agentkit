## Writing Rules

At a high-level, write an engaging story that flows and is easy to follow along. Your job is to lead an ignorant viewer through clear logical chains to the _"inescapable conclusion"_. 

## 1. Study the source material

Make sure you understand the underlying source material, check every example is realistic, and that claims check out.

## 2. Construct realistic examples 

Use examples to hang the story off. Remember to use the same example for the whole video.

## 3. Take the viewer on a journey

The video is for someone who does not know the subject matter. To enlighten them you need to introduce the status quo, point out the problem, take that problem to its dire conclusion, then resolve the tension with the solution.

## 4. Leave things out

Keep information that the viewer needs to follow the story.

## 5. Use concrete descriptions

Don't describe something in the abstract, describe the real thing directly. 

## 6. Signpost where you are going

"As an aside...", "For example...", "Problem 1: ...", "For users of the X package...."

## 7. Start each section with its subject

Bring dominant subjects to the start of explanations. Do not treat verbs as subjects.

## 8. Introduce a concepts before relying on them

Make sure that the user knows what you're talking about before you rely on a term, object or concept to explain something else.

## 9. Keep referring to the noun

Replace nouns with a pronoun or ordinals judicously. It's ok to repeat the noun to make sure the viewer doesn't lose track of the subject which is in play. 

## 10. Name who does what

Be explicit about who is the actor/agent.

## 11. Avoid slogans; explain progresively

What is important is that the user _gets_ the concept. Explaining something in complete detail misses the point and bores the viewer. Meanwhile, reducing something to a slogan or summary has no teaching value. Explain concepts brick by brick building until a mental model has been constructed. 

## 12. Lean into reductio ad absurdum 

When the story needs something to go wrong, let it go really wrong.

## 13. Write for the ear

In text, syntax highlighting tells the reader that something is an identifier. In speech, we need to use verbal punctuation.

## 14. Say the role, show the name

Long identifiers are hard to follow when spoken. Say what the thing is, and put its name in a footnote.

## 15. Use tense to separate what exists from what will exist

Use the present tense for the status quo, and the future tense for what is being proposed. If the problem and the fix both use the present tense, the listener cannot tell which one you are talking about.

## 16. Substantiate callbacks

When making a callback to a previous section, make sure to remind the user the substance of what you are referring back to.

## 17. Build tension and resolution

Make clear transitions from problem to solution, or from status quo to change, and ensure the user is aware which you are talking about.

Use a range of explicit conjunctive words to express tension or join steps within a causal chain.

Once the tensions are unbearable, land on the resolution to those tensions.
